Bond, Fund and Collateral Tokenization: Three Different Lifecycles

Debt service, NAV issuance and encumbered assets on shared infrastructure

Executive abstract

Tokenized bonds, fund interests and pledged collateral are sometimes grouped as homogeneous RWA tokens. They are not homogeneous financial claims. Bond obligations have interest and maturity schedules; fund shares have NAV and dealing rules; collateral entails priority, valuation, substitution and enforcement. This report distinguishes their rights models and develops a common event-and-assurance infrastructure.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Bond tokenization does not erase debt-service obligations

The BIS 2025 government-bond tokenisation bulletin assesses opportunities and continuing infrastructure constraints. A bond token remains a claim against an issuer whose principal, coupon, ranking, covenants and remedy depend on enforceable documentation. Token issuance speed does not guarantee improved liquidity or automatic performance of the issuer's obligations.

E02

Fund lifecycle has transfer-agent and NAV dependencies

Project Guardian's asset-management materials examine fund subscriptions, redemptions, registers and servicing. Investors generally acquire and redeem fund units according to fund terms and NAV calculations rather than continuous independently executable token pricing. A receipt token or fund token should make dealing dates, price uncertainty and fund-administrator corrections explicit.

E03

Collateral is a security interest, not necessarily fresh issuer capital

The secured party may obtain an enforceable pledge while the original owner retains relevant economic exposure. An electronic lock can restrict transfers but does not alone establish lien perfection, priority, eligibility or recovery rights. The FSB's tokenisation-risk analysis and BIS settlement prototypes show why cross-institution coordination is necessary when collateral and payment obligations are represented in different systems.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Three separate authoritative ledgers

A bond registry records face principal, series, holder identity, coupon accrual and maturity. A fund's transfer agent records shares, subscription/redemption status, class rights and NAV. A collateral register captures pledgor, secured party, asset, lien, haircut, release and enforcement. The token representation can share technical infrastructure while preserving these business-state distinctions and separate controls.

M02

Money and asset movements have different meaning

Primary bond placement funds the issuer; a tokenized fund subscription increases fund assets and share supply; a collateral pledge usually changes asset availability and priority without increasing the underlying security issuer's capital. Secondary trades generally pay the holder selling an existing instrument. Financial reports should not consolidate all four as gross capital formation.

M03

Calculation and settlement layers

Bond coupon = contractual principal × coupon rate × period fraction. A fund investor's redeemed cash depends on valid unit balance, dealing NAV and fees. Eligible collateral value depends on mark-to-market, haircut and credit policy. DvP may exchange a bond against money; collateral substitution requires secured party acceptance before old pledged assets are released. One generic 'token transfer' function cannot establish those conditions.

M04

Distinguish demonstration from adoption

BIS and Project Guardian projects illustrate technical patterns, not uniform market readiness. Compare cost, legal uncertainty, operational roles, on-chain privacy, dispute resolution and liquidity supply against conventional systems. Where tokenization may reduce reconciliation friction, measure end-to-end operations rather than individual blockchain throughput.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Worked bond coupon and pledged position

Hypothetical bond principal USD 2 million, 4% annual coupon paid quarterly. A holder pledges USD 500,000 face value as collateral, with an illustrative 10% haircut and unchanged assumed market price.

EventActor / basisFinancial movementRecord or control
Issue bondInvestors / issuer$2,000,000 new borrowingDebt and registered creditor positions created
Pay quarterly couponIssuer / paying agent$2,000,000 × 0.04 / 4 = $20,000Record-date allocation and journal
Pledge some holdingsHolder / secured party$500,000 face encumberedRestricted ownership/pledge record; bond supply unchanged
Apply haircutCollateral manager0.90 × $500,000 = $450,000Assumed eligible collateral value, not face-value guarantee

Calculation / invariant

Quarterly contractual coupon = 2,000,000 × 4% ÷ 4 = $20,000. Haircut-adjusted collateral = 500,000 × (1 − 10%) = $450,000 under the stated assumptions.

An issuer's debt, a holder's collateral encumbrance and a coupon payment are separate economic events despite common digital interfaces.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

RWA token systems need instrument-specific rights engines even when using reusable token and settlement infrastructure. The experimental priority is proving consistency among the asset, the obligation, the registry, the payment and the holder's enforceable rights.
