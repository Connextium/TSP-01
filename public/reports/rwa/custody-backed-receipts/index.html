Custody-Backed Securities Receipts: Issuance, Rights and Proof of Backing

Depositary structure versus native securities and synthetic exposure

Executive abstract

A receipt described as tokenized equity is meaningful only if investors know what asset backs it, which institution holds the asset, the governing holder rights, conversion ratio and enforceable exit arrangements. This report compares direct security issuance, third-party depositary receipts and synthetic exposure, then derives an independently testable custody-to-token reconciliation architecture for TSR.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

The legal model determines the claim

The January 2026 SEC staff statement explains different forms of tokenized securities and emphasizes that rights depend on the issuer and underlying legal arrangement; it is not binding law. A third-party receipt can represent an entitlement against a depositary rather than the shareholder-of-record status of the corresponding ordinary share. Token control, shareholder voting, distribution entitlements, bankruptcy priority and redemption are therefore separate disclosure and engineering requirements.

E02

Asset lifecycle extends beyond minting

Project Guardian's fund and tokenized collateral frameworks emphasize custody, share registers, servicing and enforcement across the instrument lifecycle. BIS and IMF research similarly warns that changing the digital record of an asset does not eliminate legal, liquidity or counterparty dependencies. Tokenization may improve coordination, but a valid asset and investor remedy must exist independently of a smart contract's balance map.

E03

A proof-of-backing must include the asset population

Confirming that a token contract has 10,000 units outstanding does not demonstrate that a custodian actually holds the eligible assets. Supply integrity requires an external asset inventory, legally segregated and unencumbered positions, issuer and custodian obligations, and a reconciliation of pending issuance and redemption. A successful on-chain event is only one piece of this chain of evidence.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Define custody and record-of-rights boundaries

Identify the underlying security, legal owner, custodian account and whether the asset may be lent or rehypothecated. Distinguish shareholder of record from holder of the contractual receipt. Store signed custody attestations and the conversion ratio in a registry independent of the token interface. Each token series should reference governing terms including voting pass-through, distributions, delivery restrictions and recovery in custodian or issuer insolvency.

M02

Quantitative backing invariant

Let U be eligible underlying units in the designated custody pool, k receipt units supported by one underlying unit, R outstanding receipts and P pending receipts reserved for issuance. A basic check is R + P ≤ kU, after adjustment for redemptions and encumbrances. Strong assurance also verifies that every U is eligible, not pledged to another obligation, and subject to enforceable rights for receipt holders. Contract-held or authorized-but-unissued tokens require explicit treatment.

M03

Primary versus secondary transaction

In a primary issue an issuer or authorized participant acquires backing assets, obtains valid custody proof, authorizes receipts and allocates them against funding. In a secondary trade an existing holder transfers receipts to another and the buyer pays the seller or intermediary; it normally does not create a new capital subscription to the issuer. The custody pool can remain unchanged even though beneficial holder records and trading balances change.

M04

Exit, redemption and failure treatment

Specify when a holder can request cash, underlying shares or only a market sale. Redemption may involve a dealing threshold, settlement cut-off, market closure or foreign jurisdiction's transfer procedures. Freeze new issue when backing is insufficient, reconcile every authorized cancellation, and preserve legally enforceable entitlements during failed token transfers or network outages. Burning receipt units before economic redemption is final can create an unrecoverable mismatch.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Synthetic fractional equity: 1,000 shares supporting 10,000 receipts

Assume a depositary acquires 1,000 eligible ordinary shares and defines 10 receipt units as the economic entitlement corresponding to one share. The numbers illustrate a proposed contract and do not establish any real instrument's legal rights.

Calculation / invariant

Backing capacity = eligible shares × 10. Before redemption 1,000 × 10 = 10,000 receipts. After 1,000 eligible receipts are redeemed and backing is proportionately released, 9,000 ÷ 10 = 900 equivalent shares.

A wallet unit may convey fractional economic rights without giving the holder direct registered share ownership. The contract and evidence must say which.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Test issuance, three secondary trades and a redemption with an independent custodian feed; no token unit may be unbacked.

  • T02

    Compare voting, dividends, insolvency and physical-redemption rights in issuer-native, receipt and synthetic securities.

  • T03

    Deliberately remove 25 underlying shares without decreasing receipt supply and require an immediate deficit alert.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

Custody-backed tokenization must make rights, backing, allocation and redemption demonstrably consistent across separate institutions. TSR should treat the custody-to-receipt reconciliation as a foundational control—not an optional administrative report.
