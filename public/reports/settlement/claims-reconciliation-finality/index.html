Accounting-Driven Settlement: Claims, Discharge and Independent Verification

Why financial-state completeness matters more than a valid transaction hash

Executive abstract

Settlement protocols need to reconcile legal obligations, instruction validity, final money transfers and independent accounts. A payment event can be cryptographically valid while failing to discharge the full contractual claim or overlooking a disputed liability. This report develops a claims-based settlement ledger that tracks authorized obligations, partial discharge, assigned creditors and independently reconstructed balances.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Financial market infrastructure is a system of obligations

The BIS/CPMI tokenisation report and IMF studies highlight why programmable financial arrangements must account for rights, money and infrastructure governance. Project Agorá and Meridian demonstrate different coordination approaches but neither eliminates the need for clear obligation identity and finality. The economic ledger must define which buyer owes what to which holder, not infer all receivables from wallet balances.

E02

Token supply cannot establish accounting completeness

On-chain transfer and burn logs describe a known population of events, not necessarily every creditor, customer deposit or off-chain asset right. FSB analysis warns of complex interdependencies in tokenized financial arrangements. Auditors need to reconcile independently sourced contracts and bank records to the token ledger. A system can be internally balanced and still omit an entire class of liabilities.

FSB24 ↗IMF25 ↗
E03

Governance and exceptions are part of finality

Account assignment, contract modification, payment netting and dispute resolution can change obligation quantities without immediate money movement. A network receiving two authenticated approvals cannot assume the newer instruction revokes the older one unless that relationship is formally defined. Recording policy versions, signers, counterparties and transaction IDs is essential to enforce release and replay rules.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Canonical obligation model

Represent each enforceable claim as ClaimID, underlying contract, debtor, creditor or beneficial holder, amount, currency, eligibility, status, assignments and maturity, linked to evidence. Separate issued claims from disputes and conditional payments. A verified payment instruction identifies the specific obligations and proportions it is authorized to discharge. This allows the same buyer obligation to be assigned among supplier, financier or multiple investors without fabricating extra principal.

M02

Accounting consistency across distinct actors

An economic settlement should generate matching counterparty entries under each institution's accounting policy, not necessarily one shared general ledger. At a simplified claim level, ApprovedOutstanding_before = ApprovedOutstanding_after + FinalSettledAmount, subject to amendments or cancellations. For every credit transfer, the sender's discharged payable should correspond to recipient claims and independently evidenced final funds; differences remain explicit exceptions.

M03

Authorize, execute, prove and reconcile

First authenticate principals and check amount, limits, consent and replay nonce. Then execute through chosen rail, collect appropriate finality proof, recognize payment discharge and update token or entitlement state. Finally reconcile claim register, token ledger and bank/payments journal. Ordering is intentional: a provisional bank acknowledgment or transient blockchain inclusion should not trigger irreversible legal discharge without an agreed rule.

M04

Completeness is an external assertion

A valid zero balance does not prove that no other invoices or claims were omitted. Assurance must include supplier statements, contract population, custody backing inventory, issuance approvals and payment-provider statements, with gaps recorded. The audit should reconstruct positions from independently controlled evidence and verify policy exception handling, rather than rely solely on the operator's own event feed.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Synthetic multi-holder partial settlement of a USD 100,000 invoice

A buyer owes USD 100,000 under a contract. After verification, USD 90,000 is approved and USD 10,000 remains disputed. Approved rights are allocated 60% to supplier and 40% to a financing institution.

Calculation / invariant

ApprovedOutstanding = 90,000 − FinalSettled. After first installment = $60,000. Creditor allocation: supplier 60% and financier 40% of each approved payment; disputed $10,000 accounted separately.

No new principal arises when the approved claim is assigned. The legal assignment, the token allocation and the monetary settlement are three linked but different events.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

Accounting-driven settlement is a richer research target than fast token movement. SPE, TRR and RPI can implement a common obligation and evidence protocol that makes incomplete financial state detectable and allows controlled recovery after cross-rail failure.
