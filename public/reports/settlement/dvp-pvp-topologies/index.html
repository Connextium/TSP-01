Delivery-versus-Payment and Payment-versus-Payment Across Ledger Topologies

Atomic platforms, synchronization operators and asynchronous hybrid settlement

Executive abstract

Settlement architecture cannot be evaluated solely by whether two smart-contract transfers execute in one block. Financial finality is the enforceable discharge of payment or delivery obligations under the relevant systems and governing law. TSP-01 compares three engineering models: a common atomic platform, a synchronization operator joining independent final systems, and an asynchronous hybrid process with contingencies. Each has different risk allocations, controls and failure modes.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Shared programmable platform

BIS Project Agorá delivered a prototype combining tokenized commercial-bank deposits with central-bank reserves on a shared programmable wholesale platform. It demonstrates a particular way of coordinating actors and money claims while retaining institutional roles and safeguards. It is not a general guarantee that placing every issuer, security and bank on one chain resolves legal settlement and governance questions.

E02

Synchronization can preserve existing infrastructure

Project Meridian and Meridian FX explore synchronizing external asset or FX systems with existing real-time gross settlement infrastructure. The core insight is that a coordinator can condition the release of legs on the other leg's state without moving every asset and currency to one ledger. This option requires trusted interfaces, precise finality semantics and procedures for unavailable or inconsistent systems.

E03

PvP and DvP mitigate but do not erase all risk

BIS analysis of FX settlement risk documents exposure when one currency is delivered irrevocably before the other. DvP similarly attempts to link securities delivery and payment, but counterparty default, market liquidity, operational outage and legal venue rules can remain material. Project Helvetia and Rialto demonstrate experimental settlement options under their own assumptions; generalizing their results to all tokenized assets would be unwarranted.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Topology A: shared atomic execution

Both delivery and payment representations execute inside one shared transaction boundary under common permission and validator policies. Rollback of an unsuccessful operation may be well defined within that platform, but the platform still needs legal recognition of its asset and money records, issuer authority, bridge/oracle validity and sufficient funds. Off-chain custody and issuer obligations can remain external.

M02

Topology B: synchronization across separate finality systems

A synchronization operator coordinates reservations, conditional locks, acknowledgments and releases across separate infrastructures—for example a tokenized securities venue and RTGS. It must know when a commitment can still be aborted and when each independent system has achieved irrevocable finality. The coordinator may reduce principal risk while introducing availability, governance and interface dependencies.

M03

Topology C: asynchronous hybrid with recovery

One leg may travel via a bank rail and the other through a DLT system, with no native cross-system atomicity. Escrow, conditional delivery, liquidity buffers, timeout policies or compensating transactions can mitigate exposure. A reversal of an already-final bank settlement may be legally impossible, so a compensation process may create a new financial obligation instead of undoing the original event.

M04

What counts as completion

A completed exchange requires matching eligible parties, valid instruction, acceptable instrument and amount, final payment under its rules, final asset delivery and balancing journals. Do not conflate an instruction acknowledgment, tentative blockchain inclusion, RTGS payment acceptance and legally effective discharge. Each timestamp must be tied to its originating authority.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.

Calculation / invariant

PvP principal exposure is bounded by the amount irrevocably delivered on one leg without the other; synchronized settlement aims to reduce that interval but does not set every legal risk to zero.

Identical economic obligations can be fulfilled through distinct technical architectures with different operational and legal assurances. A 'one-click' user experience need not imply atomic financial finality.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Implement one exchange in all three topologies; classify every failure by whether none, one or both legs are final.

  • T02

    Measure peak prefunding, unreconciled positions and time-to-spendable funds without inventing performance benefits.

  • T03

    Inject coordinator restarts, delayed confirmation and duplicate callbacks, then prove no double delivery or double payment.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

SPE and RPI should compare settlement topologies with a common legal and accounting specification. The best architecture is the one that delivers provable discharge and recoverable exceptions for the actual institutions—not simply the fewest ledger transactions.
