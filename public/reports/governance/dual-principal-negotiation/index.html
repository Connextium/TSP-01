Dual-Principal Agents and Negotiated Capital-Market Terms

Independent buyer and seller mandates, counter-offers, consent and controlled DvP

Executive abstract

Buyer and seller agents can negotiate a price or financing condition while maintaining separate interests and secret reservation values. Their negotiation transcript is not itself a securities contract or final settlement. This report designs a two-principal agent protocol with bounded offers, separate policy engines, human approval and a final execution gate.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Delegation must be scoped by each institution

MAS SAFR proposes controls for agent identity, authority and consequential decisions. For a dual-principal scenario each participant should define its own signed mandate, approved instruments, price bounds, eligible counterparties, data sharing rules and execution powers. A marketplace coordinator must not silently merge a buyer's policy with the seller's or extract confidential reservation values.

E02

Negotiation and ownership are distinct economic stages

IMF tokenized-finance research and BIS project reports distinguish market arrangements, authorization and the final transfer of economic rights. A model can produce agreed indicative terms while the purchaser lacks funds, the seller lacks inventory or the recipient cannot legally hold the instrument. Those downstream gates must be checked after negotiation and before execution.

E03

Market oversight matters when software can act continuously

The FSB identifies interconnectedness and operational complexity in tokenized finance. An automated negotiation venue that permits opaque collusion, price manipulation, unbounded information sharing or unauthorized secondary transfers can amplify those risks. Keeping agent spaces independent and recording offers supports later assessment of the transaction's integrity.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Two private agents with a neutral transcript

Each principal retains its own data store, mandate version, risk limits, preferences and log. The protocol publishes only authorized offer messages with proposal IDs, instrument, amount, currency, time and expiry. The coordinator timestamps and validates message order without obtaining unnecessary private reservation values. Human operators may inspect their own side's reasoning and the public dialogue.

M02

Feasibility is multidimensional

A price can fall within the buyer's ceiling and seller's floor yet remain infeasible for settlement. Verify available units, custody control, trading eligibility, currency acceptability, DvP route, FX cutoff and counterparty authorization. Price optimizers should use a total-value model including fees and settlement risk rather than maximize a single token quoted price.

M03

Approval precedes financial commitment

Separate tentative offer, counter-offer, indicative agreement, principal approval, binding instruction and settled delivery. Both parties must approve identical versioned terms before the settlement coordinator requests action. A subsequent change in payout account, quantity or price invalidates earlier approval unless the mandate explicitly permits that change.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

Dual-principal negotiation is suitable for TSP-01 research if agent authority is explicit, parties remain independent and the final securities and monetary event obeys normal controls. AFG proposes terms; TSR, TLS and SPE establish executable reality.
