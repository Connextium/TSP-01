Financial Agents Under Signed Mandates and Runtime Governance

Agent identity, policy checks, human approvals and accountable execution

Executive abstract

An agent may optimize or propose a transaction, but it acquires no legal authority merely by producing a convincing explanation. This report specifies a bounded financial-agent architecture that checks institutional identity, permitted actions, financial state, signed mandates, approvals and settlement evidence at the last actionable boundary.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Runtime governance is becoming a distinct research practice

MAS published Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime (SAFR) in 2026, proposing industry approaches to agent authorization, oversight and decision records. This is a governance reference, not proof that an AI model is legally entitled to bind an institution. The more consequential the transfer, the more important it becomes to separate human or institutional delegation from an LLM-generated recommendation.

E02

Programmable finance increases the consequence of software actions

IMF work on tokenized finance discusses programmable infrastructures and their governance, while BIS research addresses financial transactions whose settlement and asset legs may execute within shared or coordinated systems. Software that can submit a valid payment can produce immediate financial effects; the runtime must therefore revalidate counterparty, amount, price, assets, approval conditions and available funds independently of the agent's textual reasoning.

E03

Risk cannot be outsourced to model explanations

The FSB's analysis of tokenization identifies operational risk and financial interconnections. Those considerations extend to autonomous workflows that combine APIs, market data, internal ledgers and issuer systems. A narrative audit log without authenticated policy versions, input provenance and real settlement evidence is inadequate to reconstruct why an economically relevant transaction occurred.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Segregate authority from reasoning

Define a signed mandate containing the principal, maximum ticket, cumulative limits, permitted instruments, counterparties, settlement rails, price tolerance, time window and whether human approval is mandatory. An agent may recommend or negotiate within these bounds, but the actual execution gateway checks the mandate against independent state. Read-only research tools and privileged transaction tools should not share indistinguishable credentials.

M02

Bind consent to exact financial fields

Human approval must identify the amount, currency, payment destination, instrument, quote version, deadline and proposal hash. A materially changed transaction requires a new approval. Revoking the agent's authority or ending the window must block execution even if the model remains active. The model cannot grant itself larger limits or reinterpret policy text as permission.

M03

Close the evidence chain

Store the proposed action, signed mandate, validation decision, authorized approval, execution request, provider acknowledgment, finality proof and accounting event under a common economic ID. Distinguish blocked proposals from actual executed transfers. Exception recovery should retain the original event and any corrective entry; do not erase mistakes by editing an LLM transcript.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Attempt to alter destination and amount after approval; reject changed transactions.

  • T02

    Revoke authority before a scheduled instruction and verify the gateway blocks it.

  • T03

    Rebuild a completed execution from signed authorization to final bank and journal events.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

AFG should study runtime institutions, not unrestricted agents. Safe financial autonomy needs revocable mandates, deterministic control at execution and independently reconstructed outcomes.
