Redemption and Spendability Boundaries in Asset-Backed Transactions

Why token receipt, cash availability and discharge must be measured separately

Executive abstract

In an asset sale or redemption, the seller's goal is normally not to receive a particular blockchain event but to obtain spendable proceeds under the agreed currency and contract. This briefing distinguishes token redemption, intermediary acceptance, commercial-bank credit, liquidity access and final accounting discharge. Deep stablecoin macroeconomics are intentionally outside scope.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Stablecoin transfers cannot be equated to settled cross-border payments

BIS Working Paper 1385 compares cross-border costs among bank, fintech and stablecoin rails using harmonized all-in cost measures. Its framing makes clear why funding, conversion, withdrawal and intermediary services matter alongside network transfer. TSP-01 should preserve that insight without treating stablecoins as the subject of an independent macroeconomic programme.

E02

Redeemability is different from transferability

The BIS 2026 monetary report discusses trade-offs in digital-money arrangements, including stablecoins. An instrument may move quickly on a blockchain while access to issuer redemption, bank processing or another currency is limited. For a custody-backed stock receipt, this distinction also appears when a user can sell a token continuously but cannot redeem it into underlying shares outside specified cut-offs.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Define three endpoints

The first endpoint is transfer confirmation: an amount of a digital instrument moved under ledger rules. The second is contract settlement: the transaction counterparty has received the agreed form of payment with legal discharge. The third is economic usability: a beneficiary can apply the funds for its intended purpose. These can coincide or be hours apart; report all three rather than compressing them into a single label 'instant'.

M02

Cost and timing reconciliation

The payment process must account for initial principal, spread, network fees, intermediary fees, conversion and tax where applicable. Assign a unique beneficiary entitlement ID, expected net amount, applied FX quote, final amount and usable-funds timestamp. Once a payout is complete, reconcile token burn or asset delivery separately from cash receipt so a missing off-ramp does not disappear from reporting.

M03

Stress requires failure-aware states

Model on-chain funds available but off-ramp suspended; bank creditor account frozen; FX liquidity exhausted; payment accepted but proceeds blocked. The issuer should identify whether a claimant holds money, a receivable against a payment agent, or an unpaid distribution obligation, and should not represent any of these conditions as a completed cash return.

03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Illustrative redemption of a USD 50,000 receipt position

A holder redeems units for USD 50,000 gross. In one modeled route a bank payment carries USD 35 fees; in another a token-based payout has USD 2 network fee, 0.12% conversion spread and USD 20 off-ramp charge. These figures are synthetic.

Calculation / invariant

Synthetic net bank route = $50,000 − $35 = $49,965. Synthetic digital route = $50,000 − $2 − $60 − $20 = $49,918. Route choice depends also on risk, execution certainty and timing.

Do not generalize these illustrative fees into market averages. They show why network costs are not sufficient for comparing settlement alternatives.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Report time to transfer, time to legal settlement, and time to spendable proceeds separately under two payout routes.

  • T02

    Reconcile paid, unclaimed and pending proceeds to outstanding redemption liabilities.

  • T03

    Simulate an interrupted off-ramp and verify that holder rights and cash payables are not silently extinguished.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

For TSP-01, settlement instruments matter where they affect tokenized asset rights and completed capital returns. A future monetary-systems edition can study reserve economics and systemic monetary effects independently.
