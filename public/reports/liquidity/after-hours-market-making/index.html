After-Hours Tokenized Securities: Basis, Market Makers and Liquidity Risk

Why 24/7 receipt trading can deviate from an underlying exchange's last share price

Executive abstract

A tokenized equity receipt may trade while its underlying share exchange is closed. Its price is then determined by executable demand, order-book depth, dealer inventory, reference-price expectations and redemption access—not by a live underlying market price. This report explains premiums, discounts, market-maker inventory responses and reopening gaps, and separates observed market prices from custody-backed entitlement.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Fragmented trading hours create basis and valuation risks

The FSB's report on tokenisation highlights market fragmentation, valuation and liquidity concerns when the new market and reference market differ operationally. Token transfer capability at all hours does not provide a guaranteed two-sided market for the receipt, nor a way to sell or redeem the underlying at a fixed last-traded price. Asset and custody rights may be stable while a venue's market price changes considerably.

E02

Liquidity provision is a funded business

Market makers may hold token and underlying inventories, hedge via related instruments where permitted, or seek authorized creation and redemption to offset imbalances. An AMM substitutes pool mechanics for some quoting functions but remains exposed to inventory, price movements and adverse selection. Mariana demonstrates hypothetical wCBDC AMMs technically, not an assured securities-market arbitrage business. Neither mechanism guarantees a tokenized receipt will trade at its reference-equivalent price.

E03

Investor rights still depend on the securities structure

The SEC staff's tokenized securities statement distinguishes structures in which token buyers hold different legal claims. If an issuer-backed receipt is not freely redeemable into an underlying share, its market maker cannot rely on exact share-delivery arbitrage. If trading is restricted by eligibility or transfer-agent controls, a seeming basis opportunity may remain economically inaccessible to many investors.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Define the reference-equivalent value

Let P be the reference share price in its local currency, FX the conversion into receipt quote currency and k the receipts per share. ReferenceEquivalent = P × FX / k, adjusted for differing dividends, fees or contract rights. Basis = (ReceiptPrice − ReferenceEquivalent) / ReferenceEquivalent. During an exchange closure P is stale; report its last update and distinguish an indicative fair value from official closing price and executable receipt prices.

M02

Dealer choice to buy or sell

If receipts trade at a premium, a dealer with lawful inventory and ability to create receipts might sell additional units against sufficient backing, subject to spread, borrowing and authorization. If receipts trade at a discount, a dealer able to redeem might purchase them and recover underlying value. Without those channels, price convergence may not occur. Inventory limits and exposure to reopening gaps can be more important than marginal network transfer costs.

M03

Market data and user protection

Show last-traded receipt price, executable bid and ask, displayed order-book depth, reference market close timestamp, conversion ratio, FX timestamp and a warning on unavailable redemption. A gap at next-day open changes mark-to-market portfolio value, not the quantity of receipts held. A dealer intervention changes price through executed supply and demand, not through a privileged authority to change the underlying reference security price.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Simulate premium, discount, reopening-up and reopening-down cases with dealer inventory and a finite cash budget.

  • T02

    Measure spread and executable depth across hours; do not infer liquidity from transaction frequency alone.

  • T03

    Model a market maker unable to redeem due to bank holiday or corporate-action lock; test how basis persists.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

Tokenized securities can be technically transferable after hours without offering reference-price parity or guaranteed liquidity. TSR and TLS should provide transparent valuation, investor-rights disclosure and constrained market-making models rather than promise seamless arbitrage.
