The Total Landed Cost of Cross-Border Tokenized Capital

FX quotation, slippage, prefunding, network charges and usable beneficiary proceeds

Executive abstract

An apparently low-cost digital transfer can be expensive once acquisition, FX conversion, market depth, intermediary charges, regulatory processing, off-ramp and liquidity financing are included. This report proposes a corridor-level total-landed-cost framework for tokenized securities subscriptions, redemptions and cross-border value circulation. It distinguishes experimental route comparisons from measured market evidence.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

BIS research measures costs across complete routes

BIS Working Paper 1385, published October 2026, compares banks, fintech money-transfer operators and stablecoin rails using harmonized measures across currencies and transfer sizes. Its key design contribution for TSP-01 is a disciplined comparison of full payment paths instead of the cheapest step on any one rail. Crypto market settlement fees alone do not establish total beneficiary costs, and a fintech route may be more competitive in some corridors.

E02

FX liquidity and execution are separate from payment transport

Project Mariana experimentally tested automated market makers among hypothetical wholesale CBDCs, while Rialto examined FX-related frictions in cross-border retail payment settlement. These are useful architectures for liquidity discovery but do not imply that any particular stablecoin AMM provides deep, executable and regulated FX conversion in real capital-market corridors. Quote validity, balance-sheet inventory, settlement currencies and market access remain real constraints.

E03

Incomplete PvP exposes market participants

The BIS 2026 FX settlement risk research reviews exposures when one currency is irrevocably delivered without the other leg's delivery. Low quoted FX spreads do not compensate for an unmitigated principal loss in a default case. For a tokenized asset subscription, a currency conversion may be complete on one system while the securities acquisition is still pending; the liquidity and finality clocks must be tracked independently.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Complete cost and outcome function

For notional N, compare a rate-adjusted output rather than simply sum nominal fees across currencies. A normalized approximation in basis points is TotalCost = FX spread + price impact + network/ramp fees + intermediary charges + financing cost + expected risk/failed-transfer cost. In absolute currency: N × (spread + slippage + funding-bps)/10,000 plus fixed fees. Use executable quotes at a given depth and a defined timestamp. A posted midpoint is not an executable price.

M02

Separate funding and liquidity provisioning

A financial institution can prefund a beneficiary account, borrow intraday liquidity, hold inventory of a settlement asset, or synchronize a delivery to reduce funding duration. Each changes capital occupancy and risk allocation. Measure maximum intraday cash reserved, average holding time, financing cost, failed-payment share and beneficiary credit time. A reduction in operational time does not imply a reduction in finality risk unless both legs' assurance improves.

M03

Route decisions depend on beneficiary context

The best route for a USD subscription into a regulated issuer may differ from the best method to return local-currency proceeds to investors. Some investor or recipient accounts cannot receive stablecoins or central bank money directly; a token-based intermediate settlement may require a licensed off-ramp. Compliance access, holiday calendars, provider concentration, refund routes and documented exception costs belong in the route score.

Calculation / invariant

Total landed cost ≈ N × ((spread_bps + slippage_bps + funding_bps)/10,000) + fixed charges. Digital example: 100,000 × (8+12)/10,000 + 0.40 + 20 = $220.40.

Under these invented inputs the fintech transfer costs less than the digital-assisted route even though the digital network fee is only $0.40. Risk and elapsed time could still change route preference.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Measure three live-test corridors using executable quotes at fixed ticket sizes and preserve provider, timestamp, funding and beneficiary assumptions.

  • T02

    Stress AMM depth by reducing liquidity and evaluating price impact alongside fixed provider quotes.

  • T03

    Compare synchronized and asynchronous payment legs with peak prefunding, beneficiary spendability and settlement risk as separate outputs.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

TLS should evaluate FX as a complete investment or return workflow, not a cheap network-transfer feature. The most useful measure is net spendable value at a verifiable settlement endpoint, accompanied by actual liquidity and risk requirements.
