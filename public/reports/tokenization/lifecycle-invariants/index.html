Issue, Mint, Transfer, Redeem and Burn: A Verifiable Token Lifecycle

State machines, event correlation, disputed positions and independently reconstructed supply

Executive abstract

A token lifecycle spans more than the familiar mint and burn entry points. It begins when a legally valid economic position is originated and authenticated, and it ends only when the position has been extinguished or validly transferred out of the modeled system. This report defines a state-machine approach with explicit preconditions, durable evidence, and invariants for cross-system completeness.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Lifecycle coverage is a documented infrastructure problem

The BIS Project Promissa demonstrates why financial commitments need issuance, modification, encashment and archival states rather than a single transferable token. Project Guardian's institutional material addresses the full lifecycle of funds, including on-chain registers and asset servicing. IMF analysis on market inefficiencies similarly assesses tokenization across issuance, trading and post-trade functions. These sources support the lifecycle approach, but TSP-01's exact state machine is a design proposal, not a property proven by the cited prototypes.

E02

Token confirmations do not prove economic completeness

Smart-contract event receipts show that specified code executed under the chain's assumptions. They do not show that all relevant off-chain obligations were included, that no custodian asset was withdrawn, or that every legal transfer was authorized. The FSB identifies operational, liquidity and interconnectedness vulnerabilities. For a rights-bearing token, verification must also include source completeness, permissible state transitions and the ledger of extinguished positions.

FSB24 ↗CPMI24 ↗
E03

Operations need compensation as well as reversal

In a hybrid payment system, the money leg and the token leg may have different finality rules and recovery options. A token burn may be irreversible while a payment instruction is still rejectable, or vice versa. A recovery protocol must therefore distinguish an aborted workflow, a compensated financial event and a legal claim for restitution. Simply retrying a timed-out transaction can create duplicate payment or double release.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Formal states and guards

Model an instrument through Draft, Verified, Issued, Outstanding, Restricted, PendingRedemption, Redeemed and Cancelled states, with Disputed and Suspended as cross-cutting conditions. A successful mint requires a verified asset or claim and authorization to create supply. Transfer requires eligible recipient, valid mandate and transferable ownership. Redeem requires an accepted cancellation instruction and an agreed rule for when the economic claim is discharged. Burn is not automatically equivalent to lawful redemption.

M02

Three independent ledgers

Maintain an economic-rights ledger (instrument and legal holders), a token-state ledger (balances, contracts and on-chain events) and a settlement/accounting ledger (money movement, liabilities and journals). Reconcile them with a shared economic-event ID. The issuer cannot certify population completeness by checking token supply alone; auditors need independently sourced claims, asset backing or custodian statements against which token balances are measured.

M03

Partial settlement and partitions

Partition available rights into freely transferable, encumbered, disputed, restricted, pending-redemption and redeemed quantities. Rights should never be double-counted across partitions. For payment claims, only approved balances may become freely transferable tokenized claims. For custody-backed receipts, blocked holdings must remain represented in custody and legal rights even if tokens are transferred into an escrow or freeze state.

M04

Recovery without fabricating finality

Use idempotent instructions and a unique settlement ID, persistent state transitions, measured confirmation timeouts and explicit incomplete states. When a bank rail confirms payment but the token ledger fails, it may be inappropriate to reverse the bank entry; recovery can require issuing a legally authorized replacement token or initiating compensation. Each exception must preserve the audit trail rather than rewriting history.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Worked ledger: a partially redeemed tokenized bond

A synthetic debt instrument has USD 1,000,000 issued principal represented by 10,000 units of USD 100 each. USD 200,000 is pledged as collateral; a USD 150,000 redemption is proposed from unencumbered holdings.

EventActor / basisFinancial movementRecord or control
Issue and verifyIssuer + agent1,000,000 economic principal10,000 units issued after backing/terms approved
Encumber collateralBorrower + secured party200,000 pledged2,000 units restricted; cannot transfer freely
Redeem unencumberedIssuer + payer150,000 principal retired1,500 units cancelled against final settlement
Reconcile outstandingIndependent verifier850,000 principal still due8,500 token units outstanding; 2,000 pledged remain within outstanding supply
Release security laterSecured party200,000 pledged releasedRestriction cleared; supply unchanged
Calculation / invariant

Principal outstanding = issued principal − legally completed redemptions = 1,000,000 − 150,000 = 850,000. Outstanding units = 8,500, including 2,000 restricted units.

Restrictions change the availability of units but do not necessarily change economic outstanding principal. Burning a restricted unit without the secured party's authority could destroy token state while leaving a legally enforceable security interest.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

The invariant that matters is not that tokens can be burned: every action must preserve the relationship among legal rights, custody or liability backing, transferability and final monetary discharge. The canonical lifecycle should be a reusable foundation for TRR, TSR and SPE across the programme.
