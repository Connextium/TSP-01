The Token Is Not the Right: Registry Authority and Economic Entitlement

Separating economic claims, authoritative records, digital representations and settlement evidence

Executive abstract

Tokenization is frequently presented as a single act of digitizing ownership. Financially, it consists of several legally and operationally distinct changes: an enforceable claim arises, a registry establishes the holder, a digital representation is issued, and a transfer or redemption changes the valid claim population. The proposed architecture makes those records explicit and independently reconcilable. It compares native on-ledger assets, custodian-backed receipts and derivative-like tokens without presuming that a wallet balance is legal title.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

What the published literature establishes

The BIS/CPMI 2024 tokenisation report approaches tokens as representations of money and other assets within programmable arrangements, while the IMF's 2025 framework evaluates market frictions over the full asset lifecycle. Neither definition makes a particular blockchain balance a universal substitute for legal ownership, custody, counterparty consent, or enforceable investor remedies. The FSB's 2024 assessment highlights legal and operational vulnerabilities that arise when the represented asset, venue, issuer and related intermediaries remain economically interconnected. Accordingly, a tokenization proposal should identify which record is authoritative at each transition and in each jurisdiction.

E02

Security ownership can differ from token control

The January 2026 SEC staff statement distinguishes tokenized securities arrangements by their issuer and intermediaries. An issuer-sponsored token may carry rights under a governing securities framework; a third-party representation may instead be an entitlement against another actor, and a synthetic exposure need not confer ownership in the referenced share. The staff statement is not binding law. The design implication for TSP-01 is architectural: do not infer from a cryptographic transfer that the legally required transfer agent, depository, custodian or beneficial-ownership register has changed.

E03

Registry authority and supply completeness

Token standards primarily specify contract interfaces and transfers; they do not determine the legal priority of the record of ownership. For a depositary receipt the custody account may be an independent source of backing, while for a loan claim the signed contract and servicing ledger may dominate. TSP-01's TRR should therefore keep separate immutable identifiers for obligation, economic owner, eligible quantity, token contract, settlement instruction, disputed quantity and redemption. Events must reconcile across these records without requiring every internal document or personally identifying fact to become public.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Four-layer reference model

Layer 1 is the legally enforceable obligation or asset, identified by the issuing instrument and governing terms. Layer 2 is the authoritative rights register that determines permitted holders, units, encumbrances and restrictions. Layer 3 is the token representation, which can be held, transferred or constrained according to an interface standard. Layer 4 is the settlement and accounting evidence that establishes discharged obligations and treasury movements. Cross-layer links should be versioned and independently re-computable, including when an upstream asset record changes.

M02

A useful supply invariant

Define E as approved outstanding eligible economic interests, M as outstanding represented token units normalized to the economic denomination, and X as explicitly disclosed unrepresented interests. The design goal is E = M + X after accounting for partial settlements, redemption, disputed interests and the unit-conversion ratio. A stronger audit must also prove population completeness: all issuer obligations are included in E, not merely that the tokens already known to the auditor balance. A token smart contract cannot establish that its own issuer has declared every external liability.

M03

Native issuance, receipt, and derivative architectures

A native security may use the ledger as a legally authorized issuance and transfer record if the applicable law and governing rules permit it. A receipt architecture requires an issuer/custodian obligation backed by actual holdings and a redemption process. A derivative or synthetic token may create an exposure measured by an external price without conveying the underlying share. Compare the same user interface across these structures and the rights will differ substantially: voting, dividends, insolvency priority, collateral reuse and exchange privileges cannot be assumed equivalent.

M04

Event identity and accounting boundaries

Every registry mutation should reference the originating contract or corporate action, evidence of authorization, affected beneficial or legal holders, and the journal event if value has moved. Use separate event IDs for economic approvals, mint decisions, contract transfers, settlement finality and redemption, linked by a correlation ID. This avoids equating the token transaction hash with the entire commercial event and allows an auditor to reconstruct omissions and duplicates across systems.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Worked case: verified receivable tokenization

Synthetic invoice of USD 100,000; USD 90,000 approved and USD 10,000 disputed. The approved amount is split 60% to a supplier and 40% to a financier. All amounts are illustrative and the claim is not a negotiable security merely by being tokenized.

EventActor / basisFinancial movementRecord or control
Contract and verificationBuyer / verifier100,000 invoice; 90,000 eligibleApproved claim record excludes 10,000 disputed
Allocate eligible rightsSupplier / financier54,000 supplier; 36,000 financierTwo holder entitlements sum to 90,000
Represent eligible rightsIssuer / registry90,000 normalized claim unitsSupply linked to eligible rights, not original invoice
Settle first installmentBuyer / settlement operator30,000 paid; 18,000 supplier, 12,000 financierToken claim reduced to 60,000; signed finality receipts
Close remaining approved amountBuyer / issuer60,000 paid; 36,000 supplier, 24,000 financierEligible claim becomes zero; 10,000 dispute remains separate
Calculation / invariant

Approved rights = 90,000; initial represented rights = 54,000 + 36,000. After first installment: 90,000 − 30,000 = 60,000 outstanding. Disputed 10,000 is never automatically minted.

The exercise distinguishes an invoice, an approved payment obligation, the claim of each holder, the representation of that claim, and a completed monetary settlement. All five can change at different times.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

The most important property of a tokenization system is not token issuance speed; it is whether every represented unit corresponds to a well-defined, enforceable, currently outstanding economic position. TRR should become the programme's shared proof-of-rights architecture, while TSR specializes it for securities and SPE closes settled claims.
