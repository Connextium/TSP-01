Capital DLC–ACTOR: The Full Journey from Commitment to Realized Return

Five stages, multiple principals, double-entry economics and independent lifecycle assurance

Executive abstract

Capital DLC–ACTOR is a framework for analyzing financial capital across institutions and technologies. Activate authorizes commitments, Channel mobilizes funding and liquidity, Transform deploys capital into enforceable assets, Orchestrate services the asset and its obligations, and Return delivers realized income or exit proceeds. This report turns the mnemonic into an auditable event-and-accounting model that distinguishes issuer capital from token turnover.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Lifecycle research gives ACTOR an analytical foundation

The IMF's 2025 framework evaluates the possible effects of tokenization on frictions over the full asset lifecycle. The BIS/CPMI report stresses the interaction among monetary claims, tokenized assets and settlement systems. ACTOR is TSP-01's proposed cross-cutting framework to bridge these observations; it is not a published standard or a proven financial-market model. Its value depends on expressing concrete rights and cash flow invariants rather than simply renaming existing operational steps.

E02

Financing is more than funding a wallet

BIS Project Agorá, Meridian and Guardian research distinguish money instruments, payment rails, legally enforceable instruments and shared infrastructure. Capital can be funded through different rails without changing the identity of the asset being bought. Similarly, an asset can circulate as a token while its dividend and redemption proceeds arrive through conventional banks. ACTOR should permit mixed technology at every stage.

E03

Realized value requires more than a price chart

The FSB emphasizes financial vulnerabilities that can persist or intensify with tokenized assets, including liquidity and operational risks. A marked portfolio value is not cash realized by the holder. ACTOR's Return phase should require a cash distribution, enforceable redemption, or completed asset sale with final settlement evidence. For different investor classes, net value also reflects fees, taxes, FX and issuer recovery terms.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

A — Activate: identify capital commitments and obligations

Define the party committing capital, legal capacity, investment authorization, permitted assets, timing and maximum amount. Generate a signed mandate and subscription or funding obligation, linked to investor identity and suitability. Do not record capital as received solely because a contract was signed. For tokenized receivables, Activate may instead establish a buyer's approved obligation and a financier's contractual acquisition of a claim.

M02

C — Channel and T — Transform

Channel records how funding crosses bank, FX, stablecoin or other payment interfaces, including precise time to spendable settlement and all fees. Transform documents the legally effective asset purchase, security issue, claim assignment, token representation, and issuer balance-sheet or fund accounting. One actor may perform both steps, but capital and entitlement evidence remain separate.

M03

O — Orchestrate: preserve financial state over time

Orchestrate maintains registers, custody, secondary trade entitlements, agent governance, reconciliations, coupon or dividend schedules and exception recovery. The lifecycle should support disagreement among parties and delayed or incomplete payment instructions. A single immutable chain does not capture all economically relevant records; the system therefore creates cross-system event references and independent accounting proofs.

M04

R — Return: distinguish valuation from receipt

Return is triggered when income, redemption proceeds, liquidation cash or exit-sale consideration has become the legally or operationally available value defined in the instrument terms. A secondary trade can provide a return to a selling holder while no funds return from the issuer to shareholders. Measure net received value and remaining outstanding rights, not just market-to-market gain.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Illustrative complete investment-to-cash cycle

A fund vehicle raises USD 1,000,000, spends USD 20,000 in disclosed fees and purchases USD 980,000 in eligible securities. It later receives an USD 8,000 cash distribution and returns it in full to holders; a separate secondary trade occurs between two investors.

Calculation / invariant

Issuer/fund simplified cash flow: $1,000,000 received = $980,000 asset cost + $20,000 fees. Later $8,000 distribution payable is reduced by $8,000 upon final payout. Secondary $25,000 volume is not added to initial capital raised.

ACTOR is a classification and evidence framework. The system still needs instrument-specific legal rights, investor taxes, collateral and custody records, and validated accounts.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Design a cross-system ACTOR event schema linking investor, issuer, custodian, ledger, bank and market maker IDs without leaking private personal data.

  • T02

    Run capital-flow conservation checks under refund, failed FX settlement, fee discrepancy and partial dividend cases.

  • T03

    Model a secondary trade and a primary subscription occurring on the same day; classify their cash and asset flows separately.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

ACTOR can become TSP-01's common research grammar if every stage has defined actors, economic claims, state-transition evidence and reconciliation identities. Its purpose is not to turn all capital activity into tokens, but to make financial transformation intelligible and independently testable.
