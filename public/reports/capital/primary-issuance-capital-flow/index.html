Primary Issuance: From Investor Funding to Verified Tokenized Assets

Capital subscriptions, issuer treasury, acquisition, custody and receipt issuance

Executive abstract

Primary tokenized issuance is an economic chain, not a mint operation. Investor money is committed, received, converted and allocated; the issuer or vehicle acquires an underlying asset or creates a lawful obligation; a rights registry recognizes holders; tokens are issued only against the authorized interests. This report designs an issuance ledger in which every unit of incoming capital can be followed to asset acquisition, fees, cash reserve and eventual investor entitlement.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Tokenization may change frictions but cannot create assets

The IMF's analysis of financial-market inefficiencies organizes tokenization effects over origination, trading and post-trade activity. A distributed ledger may streamline data sharing or reduce reconciliation, yet investors still need valid securities issuance terms, properly handled subscription money, and a legally recognizable claim. Project Guardian's implementation frameworks link fund issuance to transfer agency, custody and servicing. Therefore a capital-flow system must originate enforceable rights before it authorizes token supply.

E02

The primary versus secondary distinction is economic

At a primary issue, investment funds may increase the issuer's or fund's net assets or financing position, subject to intermediary fees and capital structure. At a later secondary trade, cash commonly moves between holders and dealers, not to the issuer. IOSCO's 2025 tokenization research and the BIS/FSB material on market development do not imply that a larger on-chain trading volume is equivalent to new financing for the real economy. TSP-01 should report issuer proceeds, net investment and secondary turnover as separate measures.

IOSCO25 ↗FSB24 ↗IMF25 ↗
E03

Settlement asset choice is a supporting design decision

A security subscription can be funded through conventional bank transfers, tokenized deposits, wholesale settlement instruments or a stablecoin-assisted conversion flow, depending on participants and law. BIS Project Agorá demonstrates a programmable wholesale prototype combining bank deposits and central bank reserves; it does not establish that private stablecoins are required for securities issuance. The economic endpoint is available and legally credited funding, not a particular coin or ledger.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Separate contractual commitment from received capital

A signed subscription document establishes an investor's commitment but should not be booked as cash received until actual bank or settlement-system evidence arrives. Assign a subscription ID, investor eligibility, investment amount, currency, allocation limit and refund policy. Funding messages should be correlated to the subscription without treating wallet or bank account balances as universally final. An oversubscription, rejected investor or late FX delivery must enter an explicit pending/refund state.

M02

Issuer, broker and custodian cash accounts

Track gross subscriptions, processing or placement fees, conversion costs, reserve cash, and actual cash deployed into underlying assets. Use separate journals for the issuer or fund, the paying broker, and the custodian. On-chain tokens may show claims against an issuer while the underlying securities still settle conventionally. Reconcile purchases only after the custodian or settlement agent confirms ownership or a valid receivable in the authorized legal account.

M03

Prove right-to-asset backing before mint

If the product is a custody-backed receipt, only eligible underlying assets that have been acquired and credited to the designated account support minting. If the product is a natively issued security, the authorization and issue record rather than a custody portfolio establish outstanding units. TRR must record which model is being used; TSR applies the backing invariant for depositary receipts. Inventory used for another obligation should not be silently counted twice.

M04

Complete funding and rights reconciliation

Define F gross subscriptions, K costs and fees, A net asset acquisitions, C remaining reserve or operating cash, and L net outstanding refunds/payables. Under the illustrative simplified model F = K + A + C + L. This is a cash-use identity, not a profitability equation. Separately define receipt supply R against eligible assets U and conversion k: R ≤ U × k. The two identities must reconcile through the ledger but should never be collapsed into a claim that every cash dollar directly backs an identical token value.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Worked r-SHARE-style issuance: USD 1 million subscribed

Synthetic investment vehicle receives USD 1,000,000 in subscriptions, charges USD 20,000 of disclosed processing/placement costs and acquires 4,000 underlying shares at USD 245 each. It issues 100 receipt units per underlying share. This is a modeling exercise, not a live security or guaranteed issuance price.

Calculation / invariant

Gross F = $1,000,000 = $20,000 disclosed costs + $980,000 asset acquisitions. Eligible receipt capacity = 4,000 shares × 100 = 400,000 units.

An investor's rights are defined by subscription and depositary terms; treating the receipt as a permanently fixed claim of $2.50 cash would be misleading. Fees, market moves and custodial realization alter economic value.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Reconstruct each subscription dollar into fees, settled assets, reserve cash, refunds and payable balances with no unexplained difference.

  • T02

    Reject an issuance when the broker confirms a trade but the custodian has not yet received eligible asset delivery.

  • T03

    Compare primary issuance under bank-funded, tokenized-deposit and stablecoin-assisted flows using time to spendable proceeds and legal finality.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

Capital formation should be measured at the issuer and asset-acquisition boundary, not inferred from minted token supply. The flagship report provides a common primary-market financial graph on which TSR, TRR, TLS and SPE can build measurable issuance experiments.
