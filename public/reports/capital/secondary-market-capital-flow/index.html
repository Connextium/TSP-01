REPORT 04.02II · Capital & MarketsFlagshipRESEARCH EDITION / 2026

Secondary Markets: Where Token Cash, Holder Rights and Prices Move

Holder-to-holder trading, inventory, premiums, off-hours liquidity and issuer-neutral turnover

Executive abstract

A tokenized equity receipt traded on a secondary venue can move repeatedly without the issuer raising additional capital or the custodian purchasing a new share. Investors exchange cash with sellers, market makers supply inventory and a token registry updates eligible rights. Market price may differ from a reference equity price when trading hours, redemption rights, funding access and liquidity conditions diverge. This report models these flows and the limits of arbitrage.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

A trade changes ownership, not necessarily issuer capitalization

Conventional secondary securities markets transfer existing interests among investors. Tokenizing the transfer mechanism does not convert the price paid to the seller into new issuer financing. IMF financial-market research examines issuance and trading frictions separately; the distinction should persist in transaction analytics. TSP-01's capital graph must therefore separate issuer proceeds, outstanding supply, custody inventory, holder-to-holder consideration and venue volume.

E02

Liquidity does not automatically follow 24-hour token settlement

The FSB's 2024 analysis identifies risks when tokenized markets and their references operate with different hours or liquidity. A blockchain can operate continuously while the underlying stock market, bank rails, custodians and redemption agents are unavailable. In such periods, secondary token price may incorporate uncertainty about future reference prices, FX movements, financing rates, spreads and withdrawal limits rather than track a frozen official closing price.

E03

Market makers face inventory and arbitrage constraints

A market maker can buy discounted receipts or sell premium receipts only if it has inventory, funding, delivery capability and rights to acquire or redeem the underlying. If mint/redemption is restricted to approved counterparties or delayed until an exchange opens, the arbitrage channel is incomplete. The difference between token price and reference value can persist without proving an error in the token ledger. That difference must be disclosed and monitored.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Follow four independent flows

The buyer transfers the settlement instrument to the seller or venue/clearing intermediary; the seller transfers an existing receipt to the buyer; the registrar updates legally valid entitlement or beneficial holder records; and the custodian generally retains the same underlying asset inventory. A venue can intermediate cash and token legs without receiving direct beneficial ownership, depending on clearing and custody rules. Record the legal and operational model instead of assuming wallet address equals shareholder.

M02

Reference value and secondary market basis

For k receipts per underlying share, a simple reference-equivalent value is underlying stock price P divided by k, adjusted where relevant for FX and rights differences. Let market token price T. The basis b = (T − P/k) / (P/k) can be positive or negative. It is an indicative comparison, not risk-free arbitrage profit: execution fees, spreads, custody restrictions, corporate-action rights, transfer taxes and conversion costs may remove or reverse any apparent return.

M03

Inventory, order matching and settlement risk

A market maker may quote bid and ask prices based on expected reference value, position limits and funding cost. An AMM pools assets and exposes providers to inventory and price-risk dynamics. In either case the venue must manage partial orders, cancellation, replay and delivery-versus-payment outcomes. A quote visible in an app is not necessarily an executed fill or final settlement. Distinguish last-trade price, displayed midpoint and executable quantity.

M04

Issuer reporting versus market reporting

Report primary gross proceeds, outstanding receipt supply and custodian backing separately from traded notional, bid-ask spread, price basis and number of unique economic holders. Secondary volumes can double count market maker routing or self-trades if only transfer events are observed. Identify wash activity and intermediary movements before claiming that on-chain token count equals investment adoption.

Two systems of record. Dotted vertical links require explicit legal, accounting and settlement reconciliation—not presumed on-chain equivalence.
03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Synthetic trade at a secondary premium

Assume one underlying share trades at an indicative USD 245 and ten receipts represent one share. An independent token venue quotes USD 25.50 per receipt while its reference-equivalent value is USD 24.50; illustrative amounts ignore taxes and fees unless noted.

Calculation / invariant

Secondary-market consideration = 200 × $25.50 = $5,100 paid to the selling holder. Reference-equivalent = $245 ÷ 10 = $24.50; basis = +4.08%. Issuer proceeds from this resale = $0.

A premium may persist where receipt creation/redemption, underlying market access or FX is constrained. An issuer cannot represent this $5,100 trade as new issuance capital.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Trace token and cash movements across buyer, seller, dealer, venue, custodian and issuer; assert issuer cash remains unchanged in a pure resale.

  • T02

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

Token liquidity and capital formation are related but distinct. A credible tokenized securities venue must expose market basis, economic turnover, inventory coverage, final settlement and redemption assumptions while preserving accurate reporting of where capital actually goes.
