Institutional Controls for Tokenized Asset Rights and Custody

Eligibility, segregation of duties, network permissioning, recovery and operational resilience

Executive abstract

Tokenization may provide cryptographic integrity for a specified state transition while failing to establish that the transition was permitted, legal, complete or recoverable. Institutional controls include actor identity, legal authority, investor eligibility, custody access, transaction limits, governance of upgrade keys, privacy, segregation and business-continuity procedures. This report distinguishes permissioned participation from confidentiality and defines a control model for the TSP-01 workstreams.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

The nature of risks does not disappear on a ledger

The Financial Stability Board's 2024 tokenisation assessment highlights vulnerabilities involving liquidity, leverage, interconnectedness, operational resilience and structural complexity. The BIS/CPMI work notes that new digital infrastructures require appropriate governance, credit and liquidity controls as well as legal safeguards. A valid token balance is a record of a digital state, not evidence of a valid custodian segregation arrangement or regulator-approved transfer.

E02

Tokenized securities remain securities with governing rights

The SEC's nonbinding staff statement and Trading and Markets FAQ address how security ownership, intermediaries, recordkeeping and custody may interact with DLT. These sources do not grant general permission to issue tokenized securities or dispense with transfer-agent and investor protection responsibilities. Controlled instruments must define their legal rights and operational agents before smart-contract role assignment.

SEC26 ↗SEC-FAQ ↗
E03

Security controls are broader than a token interface

ERC-7518 and other token interface proposals can specify authorization and compliance mechanisms, but contract standards alone cannot establish off-chain identity, adequacy of legal documentation, sanctions status, fiduciary duties or independent audit. A permissioned public EVM application can require eligible wallets while its transaction events remain publicly observable. Confidentiality requires a separate privacy architecture and selective data-disclosure policy.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Actor and mandate model

Define issuer, registrar, custodian, payment agent, investor, broker, trading venue, settlement coordinator and auditor as different legal and operational principals. Assign each explicit authority: issuance approval, custody attestation, eligible transfer, payout instruction, manual override, pause and forensic access. No single routine operator should be able to create outstanding financial claims, release their backing and suppress the independent audit in one transaction path.

M02

Permissioning, privacy and public liquidity

A public chain with permissioned contract access may enable transparent settlement-event evidence and public price discovery while blocking transfers to ineligible persons. Public liquidity is not assured by chain availability; eligible counterparty depth, broker integration, legal transferability and trading venue rules determine executable markets. Wallet allowlists also do not provide confidential economic detail by themselves. Keep sensitive identities off-chain or use legally appropriate disclosure technology.

M03

Upgrade and emergency power

Each mint, forced transfer, freeze, upgrade, key rotation or emergency pause needs an authorized policy version, quorum, reason, effective timestamp and independent audit artifact. A freeze can protect investors from a compromised key but can also deny property use or create governance concentration. Specify appeal, notification and recovery paths, including what may happen if the issuer, operator or custodian becomes insolvent.

M04

Operational resilience and loss containment

Test custodian outage, validator failure, lost private keys, delayed bank settlement, duplicate transaction signals, issuer bankruptcy and compliance rule changes. A valid business-continuity plan does not require pretending the ledger can reverse all external transactions. It defines pending rights, safe no-mint states, evidence preservation and an institution responsible for compensation or court-directed correction.

Calculation / invariant

Authorization rule = ValidIdentity ∧ AuthorizedRole ∧ MandateVersion ∧ TransactionLimits ∧ ValidUnderlyingRight. A valid signature is necessary but insufficient if the mandated action is prohibited.

Segregated authority makes a malicious or mistaken single actor less able to fabricate rights or hide obligations. The degree of independence must be evaluated against real organizational ownership.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Attempt unauthorized mint, blacklisted transfer and privileged upgrade using adversarial roles; verify prevention and audit evidence.

  • T02

    Run a custodian outage while investors trade on a venue and define safe issuance, transfer and redemption states.

  • T03

    Compare permissioned public EVM, private consortium ledger and centralized registry against confidentiality, auditability and liquidity-access requirements.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

The security boundary for institutional tokenization is the combination of enforceable rights, independent assets, governance authority, privacy and failure recovery. A permissioned token is only one component of that assurance architecture.
