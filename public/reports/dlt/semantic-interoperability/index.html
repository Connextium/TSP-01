Semantic Interoperability Across Securities, Cash and Accounting Ledgers

Identifiers, ISO 20022 events, authoritative state and financial consistency

Executive abstract

Interoperability is often framed as moving a token from Chain A to Chain B. Financial systems require a more demanding outcome: independent institutions must agree which obligation changed, which party became entitled, when money discharged the debt and which ledger entry represents the legally relevant event. This report proposes a semantic interoperability model across token contracts, custodians, payment rails and accounting systems.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Interlinking existing rails need not rely on a new shared ledger

The BIS Project Nexus blueprint standardizes connections between domestic instant payment systems, while Meridian explores synchronization between central bank RTGS and other infrastructure. Agorá explores a shared platform with modular national and bank roles. These examples offer competing coordination architectures rather than a single universal blockchain bridge. The design should select infrastructure based on required rights and finality, not the availability of a cross-chain protocol.

E02

Standards convey structure but do not settle legal obligations

ISO 20022 payment messages can describe participants, amounts, purpose and processing states, but data format consistency alone does not guarantee that a token transfer constitutes irrevocable payment or legal asset delivery. A financial event also requires instrument identity, governing terms and the authority establishing the completion status. The BIS/CPMI report highlights how token arrangements change institutional processes and responsibilities, not merely message transports.

E03

External audit depends on event populations

An institution might have perfectly consistent token balances while its bank's payment account contains a missing refund or its custodian records unacknowledged securities. The FSB's assessment underscores the operational risk of complex tokenized interconnections. Synchronizing message streams cannot reconstruct legally unrecorded claims without independent source evidence. TSP-01's RPI should therefore treat completeness and provenance as explicit interface guarantees.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Define a canonical economic-event envelope

Use an EconomicEventID and correlate contract/obligation ID, legal asset identifier, payer, payee, beneficial owner, currency, amount, effective time, settlement instructions, instruction status and finality authority. Carry source message and proof references separately from normalized interpretations. State transitions should be versioned and signed when they change rights; not every web or chain webhook has authority to alter the obligation.

M02

Map to systems with different semantics

ISO 20022 pacs messages may express interbank customer credit transfer or settlement instructions, and camt messages can report statements and notifications; a smart-contract Transfer log changes token balances; a bank core ledger records assets and liabilities; a custodian register maintains legal securities positions. A mapping table must document which system owns each field and which message is merely evidence. Unrecognized or ambiguous status codes should become exceptions rather than inferred success.

M03

Idempotency and causal ordering

Guarantee each economic action has a unique idempotency key, causal parent if it is a correction, replay nonce and settled/failed state version. Deliveries may be duplicated, reordered or delayed across bank and blockchain systems. Do not double count a provisional notification as a settled payment, and do not overwrite a previously final state without an explicit compensating business event.

M04

Verify end-to-end state rather than bridge messages

A useful protocol output is not simply token A burned and token B minted. It is a signed evidence package identifying the original claim, acceptance conditions, independent finality proof, completed owner or payee entitlement and corresponding accounting position. Privacy may require private proofs or permissioned access to details; transparent message identifiers should not expose sensitive investor or bank data.

03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

One USD 75,000 event across four institutions

A buyer makes a USD 75,000 approved payment to an assigned creditor while a related tokenized receivable is retired. The payment network, token ledger, rights registry and general ledger are independently operated.

Calculation / invariant

Event consistency requires one economic event ID linked to distinct system records. A mapping is complete only when all required financial-state assertions are present, not when each interface returned HTTP 200.

A token burn cannot settle the buyer's payable if the bank payment failed. Equally, a bank payment can be final before the token registry is corrected, creating a controlled operational exception.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Round-trip a sample transfer from business event to ISO 20022-like message, token event and journal with field-level provenance.

  • T02

    Reorder and duplicate messages, including a final status before an earlier provisional notification, and prove consistent outcomes.

  • T03

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

RPI should be a semantic protocol for financial obligations and independently attested outcomes. A cryptographic bridge is only one possible transport component, not a substitute for legal entitlement, rail finality or audit reconstruction.
