Bridge, Gateway and Synchronization Designs: Trust and Failure Boundaries

Cross-chain token movement versus legally effective capital-market interoperability

Executive abstract

A DLT bridge may transfer a token representation between networks, but its security depends on custody, validators, proof verification, upgrade governance and recovery processes. A financial institution may instead use a gateway to preserve the authoritative asset registry and synchronize events with an external bank rail. This report compares three design families and defines the evidence required for safe capital-market interoperation.

01 / SOURCE-BASED RESEARCH

Published evidence and limits.

Source-based observations are distinguished from TSP-01 design proposals. BIS Innovation Hub prototypes demonstrate specified experiments; they are not evidence of generalized market deployment.

E01

Multiple topologies are being tested

Project Agorá explores a shared programmable wholesale infrastructure; Meridian and Meridian FX demonstrate coordination among existing payment systems and external assets; Nexus develops standardized interlinks among instant payment systems. These architectures have different trust and governance boundaries. Financial institutions should not conclude that any one architecture is universally superior based only on a successful proof of concept.

E02

Token duplication can create distinct legal claims

A lock-and-mint bridge may create a wrapped token on a destination chain while the source asset remains locked in a contract. That arrangement produces reliance on the bridge custodian, proof mechanisms and a redemption path. The destination token is not automatically legally identical to the underlying security or an issuer-authorized representation. The FSB's concerns about complexity and interconnections are material where the bridge itself becomes an additional counterparty.

E03

Bank rail gateways have different failure semantics

When a banking interface is used, payments may become final under RTGS or bank clearing rules even if the DLT system remains unavailable. Synchronization operators are designed to coordinate such systems but require trusted status messages and operational fallback. An asynchronous bridge between financial obligations can reduce reconciliation lag without promising atomic cancellation of an already-final external payment.

02 / TSP-01 ANALYTICAL ARCHITECTURE

Mechanisms and responsibilities.

Original TSP-01 conceptual process model. It is an analytical illustration, not empirical proof of a deployed system.
M01

Lock/mint and burn/release

Source tokens are immobilized and destination representations created after verification; redemption burns or locks destination units and releases source assets. Under a simplified invariant, source assets locked must be sufficient for outstanding wrapped claims, subject to ratios and fees. The bridge must define which verifier can approve release, proof finality depth, emergency pause and failure recovery. Source-chain reorgs or validator collusion can violate assumptions.

M02

Institutional gateway and authoritative registry

A gateway may record a change in wallet control while keeping the legal holder ledger and custody system authoritative in one jurisdiction or network. This avoids creating competing wrapped supply but introduces a control-plane dependency on the issuer, registrar and network operator. The gateway must authenticate parties, enforce eligibility and prevent duplicated instructions across APIs and chain events.

M03

Synchronization operator for multi-rail delivery

A coordinator can reserve or condition asset and money legs on separately administered ledgers. Instead of inventing a new mirrored asset, it asks each system for authenticated hold/accept/finality evidence and triggers release under predetermined rules. It must document the last reversible point, the effect of operator insolvency, and what happens when one leg has become final but the other is pending.

M04

Security assurance and operability

Evaluate upgrade and key control, validator diversity, recovery duration, ledger finality, replay resistance, force-unwind policy, privacy, source-of-truth hierarchy and data retention. A bridge can be technically audited yet economically unsafe if its wrapped token exceeds eligible underlying assets. Maintain externally sourced backing and liability checks.

03 / WORKED FINANCIAL SCENARIO · SYNTHETIC

Synthetic transfer of 1,000 tokenized units between ledgers

An issuer-authorized asset representation has 1,000 transferable units on Ledger A. An intermediary proposes to make them tradable on Ledger B. The legal terms prohibit duplicating outstanding beneficial claims.

Calculation / invariant

Simplified bridge coverage = eligible locked source assets − outstanding redeemable wrapped claims. In fault case: 1,000 − 1,200 = −200 units. Negative coverage requires a controlled response, not a cosmetic price adjustment.

The same UX promise—'move token across networks'—can create very different custody and legal risks. Financial product design should begin with the authorized holder and asset backing record.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & FALSIFICATION

What needs testing.

  • T01

    Fault-inject unauthorized 20% wrapped supply increase and require early detection by independent asset coverage checks.

  • T02

    Simulate a source-chain reorganization after destination mint; evaluate the needed proof delay or rollback policy.

  • T03

    Compare bridge, gateway and synchronization against legality, completion time, liquidity lock-up and worst-case holder loss.

06 / CONCLUSION

Research implication.

DLT interoperability should preserve financial rights and auditable capital flows rather than privilege token mobility. RPI should explicitly compare trust topologies and provide a recovery protocol whenever infrastructure boundaries make atomicity impossible.
